Personality and Academic Life

(a) My Three Strongest Personality Traits and Their Impact on My Academic Life

I would describe myself as determined, curious, and independent. These traits influence how I approach my studies and the challenges I face as a Computer Science student.

Determination is one of my strongest traits. At 33, I am pursuing my degree while adjusting to living on my own and managing financial responsibilities. Balancing tuition, loan repayments, and household expenses is not always easy, but I remind myself why I started. This determination encourages me to keep studying, complete assignments, and continue working toward my goals even when I feel overwhelmed.

Curiosity is another important trait because Computer Science requires continuous learning and problem-solving. I enjoy exploring how technology works and learning how computer systems can be used to solve real-world problems. My curiosity encourages me to ask questions, research unfamiliar topics, and explore career options such as digital forensics, programming, and computer security.

Independence has become increasingly important since I moved out of the family home. I am learning to make decisions, manage my time, and take responsibility for my choices. Academically, this means taking ownership of my learning instead of depending entirely on lecturers or classmates. Although I may seek assistance when necessary, I understand that I must put in the effort to understand the material myself.

Together, these traits help me remain focused, adaptable, and responsible as I work toward completing my degree.

(b) My Personal Motto: “Fortune Favors the Bold”

My favourite motto is “Fortune favors the bold,” which I sometimes express as, “Fortune helps the brave.” Although it is a traditional saying rather than a quote from one particular hero, it reflects the attitude I want to maintain throughout my life.

To me, being bold means having the courage to pursue opportunities even when I am uncertain about the outcome. At 33, I have chosen to continue my education, explore a career in technology, and take on the responsibilities of living independently. Each of these decisions requires courage because there is no guarantee that everything will go according to plan.

What makes this motto meaningful is that it reminds me not to allow fear or self-doubt to prevent me from progressing. For example, when I face a difficult programming assignment or encounter a concept I do not understand, I can either become discouraged or challenge myself to learn it. I may not get everything right on the first attempt, but I can improve through practice and persistence.

My motto does not mean taking unnecessary risks or acting without thinking. Instead, it reminds me that preparation and effort must accompany courage. I want to apply this mindset to my studies, my future career, and my personal life.

(c) Large Language Models: A Tool, Not a Crutch

Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Microsoft Copilot, are becoming useful tools in education. To me, the statement that they must be used as a tool and not a crutch means that technology should support my learning rather than replace my own thinking. As a Computer Science student, I believe these tools can help me understand difficult concepts, but I must remain responsible for the work I submit and the knowledge I develop.

One way I can use LLMs is when I am struggling to understand a topic. For example, if I find a programming concept confusing, I can ask ChatGPT to explain it in simpler terms and provide a basic example. I can then practise writing my own code and test whether I understand the concept. LLMs can also help me brainstorm assignment ideas, organise study notes, generate practice questions, and identify possible errors in code.

However, I must recognise their limitations. LLMs can provide incorrect information, produce faulty code, or give references that do not exist. If I copy an answer without checking it, I may submit incorrect work and miss the opportunity to learn. Depending on these tools too heavily could also weaken my problem-solving skills, which are essential in Computer Science.

I believe the best time to use an LLM is after I have made an honest attempt to understand a topic or solve a problem myself. I can use it to clarify what I do not understand, compare different approaches, and check my reasoning. I should then verify important information using textbooks, academic articles, official documentation, and reliable sources. I must also follow my institution's rules on AI use, acknowledge assistance where required, and never submit AI-generated work as my own when that is prohibited.

Ultimately, I see LLMs as learning assistants rather than replacements for lecturers, textbooks, or my own effort. My goal is not simply to complete assignments but to develop the knowledge and skills needed to succeed in my future career. Using AI responsibly will allow me to benefit from technology while preserving my independence, critical thinking, and academic integrity.
