The Richest Man in Babylon - Reflection

Jump to: View 5 Laws of Gold Section

Introduction

Reading The Richest Man in Babylon has been meaningful to me because I am currently experiencing a new chapter in my life. I recently moved out of the nest and am learning what it means to manage a home and become more financially independent. At the same time, I have to save for my Computer Science tuition, repay a loan, and gradually purchase furniture and appliances for my home. These responsibilities can be overwhelming, especially when everything seems important at the same time. The book has reminded me that financial success requires discipline, patience, and wise decisions.

7 Cures for a Lean Purse

The lesson that stood out to me most was the importance of saving a portion of my income and controlling my expenses. Since moving into my own home, I have realised how quickly money can disappear. There is always something else to buy, whether it is an appliance, furniture, groceries, or another household necessity. However, I must also consider my tuition and loan repayments.

For example, I might want to purchase a refrigerator or a sofa immediately to make my house feel complete, but saving toward my tuition must also remain a priority. Instead of trying to furnish my entire home at once, I can purchase items gradually, starting with what I genuinely need. This would allow me to manage my responsibilities without putting unnecessary pressure on my finances.

The book also reminds me that investing in myself is important. Completing my Computer Science degree can open doors to better career opportunities, so I must be careful not to sacrifice my education for things I can acquire over time.

5 Laws of Gold

The five laws taught me that money must be protected and managed wisely. Having money is not enough; I must know how to use it. For me, this means thinking carefully before making purchases and avoiding the temptation to spend simply because I want my new home to look complete.

I have also learned that not every financial decision needs to happen immediately. Paying my loan, saving for tuition, and buying household essentials require planning. Rather than rushing to do everything at once, I can set priorities, make a budget, and work toward each goal gradually. I also need to be cautious about borrowing more money just to furnish my home, especially when I already have financial commitments.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway is that financial independence is not about having everything at once; it is about making wise decisions with what I have. Moving out has taught me that being independent comes with responsibilities that require sacrifice and planning. There are times when I may have to delay buying something for my home so that I can meet my tuition payments or reduce my debt.

As I continue pursuing my Computer Science degree and deciding between digital forensics, programming, and computer security analysis, I want to remain focused on building a secure future. My motto, “Fortune favors the bold,” reminds me to pursue my goals courageously, but this book has taught me that courage must be balanced with discipline and good judgement.

Ultimately, I want to build a life where I can maintain my home, complete my education, manage my finances, and remain true to my values. I have learned that I do not need to accomplish everything overnight. By making thoughtful decisions and staying consistent, I can build the future I want, one step at a time.

Reference

Clason, G. S. (1926). The richest man in Babylon.
